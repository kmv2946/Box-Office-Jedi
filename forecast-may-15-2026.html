FORECAST: ‘Michael’ to Reclaim Top Spot in Fourth Weekend

by Box Office Jedi

Thursday, May 14, 2026

Last weekend, we went out on a limb with our forecast and predicted Michael would overtake Mortal Kombat II and The Devil Wears Prada 2 in a surprise finish. It didn’t happen, but it came closer than most forecasters expected. Michael came in above our own predictions and was less than a million shy of finishing ahead of MKII.

But for this coming weekend, we find ourselves in even stranger territory, and this time with more certainty. Michael will reclaim the top spot in its fourth weekend.

Since Sunday, it’s been in the #1 spot every day this week, narrowly beating out Prada 2. This weekend, it’s getting back a chunk of the PLF screens it lost last weekend, for an IMAX encore. To further pave the way for the king, no new releases this weekend pose a real threat.

Let’s talk about this.

Michael is playing like it’s the 1980s. In the twenty-first century, especially in the post-streaming era, movies don’t casually drop in and out of the number one spot. Top Gun: Maverick is the anomaly, where in some bizarre box office phenomenon, it returned to number one on Labor Day weekend after hovering in the top 10 all summer long. That was its 15th weekend in release. True 80s behavior.

Weapons also returned to number one in its fourth weekend. And while Weapons is its own kind of box office marvel, that was August, and August is notoriously quiet, allowing for these kinds of scenarios (like Maverick in 2022). The post-holiday season enables this kind of thing too (see Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle).

But this is mid-May. There should be no room for a four-week-old movie to sneak back on top. It’s unheard of. Come to think of it, when looking solely at the May–June–July frame, the busiest three months on the movie calendar, the last time this occurred was in 1992, with Lethal Weapon 3. Finding Nemo came close in June 2003—resurfacing at number one in its third weekend.

Yes, there have been a handful of instances where big blockbusters with legs have held the number one spot for four consecutive weeks, like The Dark Knight, but non-consecutive is somehow more impressive. Because in our post-streaming age, once something isn’t the headline, it’s dust. Michael is somehow proving otherwise.

We’d have to look back to 1986, when the original Top Gun played just like Michael—opening at #1, falling out of the top spot in its 2nd and 3rd weekends, and returning to #1 in its 4th. It couldn’t be more appropriate that Top Gun will return to the top 10 this weekend alongside Michael, for its 40th anniversary reissue.

If there were a threat to Michael’s hold on the top spot, it wouldn’t be Prada 2. It’d be Obsession, if it were to break out beyond all reasonable expectations. It will break out, just likely not enough to overtake Michael. This one feels like Smile, and while we don’t think it’ll open that high ($22.6 million), it will far exceed the industry’s early projections.

Our predictions below:

1 Michael $25.0M
2 The Devil Wears Prada 2 $20.6M
3 Obsession $16.1M
4 Mortal Kombat II $12.7M
5 The Sheep Detectives $10.0M
6 Project Hail Mary $4.6M
7 The Super Mario Galaxy Movie $4.0M
8 In the Grey $3.5M
9 Top Gun $2.8M
10 Is God Is $2.2M
