FORECAST: ‘Mandalorian’ to Rule Memorial Day as Box Office Jedi

by Box Office Jedi

Thursday, May 21, 2026

With the exception of Solo: A Star Wars Story in 2018, we haven’t gotten a Star Wars movie over Memorial Day weekend since 2005, when Revenge of the Sith closed out the prequel trilogy. So there’s a glimmer of nostalgia to the release of The Mandalorian and Grogu.

From 1977 to 2005, Star Wars was a Memorial Day tentpole. The original Star Wars opened May 25, 1977. The Empire Strikes Back, Return of the Jedi, and all three prequels (The Phantom Menace, Attack of the Clones, Revenge of the Sith) followed suit, each opening in mid-to-late May. When Disney acquired Lucasfilm in 2012 and resumed production, the franchise shifted to December.

But the gaps between Return of the Jedi and The Phantom Menace, and between Revenge of the Sith and The Force Awakens, were wide enough to create massive droughts. Since The Rise of Skywalker (2019), the franchise has lived almost entirely on Disney+. The Mandalorian got three seasons. The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, Andor, Ahsoka, Skeleton Crew, The Acolyte —

Well, you know this already. There isn’t much interesting to say here.

Maybe we take this opportunity to talk about our name. Where did Box Office Jedi come from?

It’s a product of Box Office Mojo lore. It could be myth, but we have good reason to believe it’s true. It was the start of the summer season. Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me was the projected number one movie, and so Brandon Gray (founder of BOM) made a quick call to steal its “Mojo.”

But it wasn’t the first idea. He had been using another working title in the months prior, in reference to the year’s other most-anticipated film, Star Wars: Episode I — The Phantom Menace.

Hence, Box Office Jedi. Definitely more interesting.

Where this weekend gets fun is in its implications for next weekend. The question is whether Star Wars opens high enough to guarantee a second weekend at #1, or softly enough to leave the door open for Backrooms, which debuts May 31. Backrooms is a likely #2 in its first weekend, but if Star Wars’s 3-day comes in under $75 million, next weekend’s race could get interesting.

The second most interesting consideration this weekend is how small a drop Obsession will get in its second frame. It’s been doing insane weekday numbers, taking the #1 spot on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, only dropping 39% from Sunday to Monday. It’s unusual, and it’s unclear how that’ll translate to weekend numbers. If we apply a traditional weekday-to-weekend equation, Obsession would actually see an increase in its second frame, which for a contemporary horror movie is unheard of. Only The Ring comes to mind. At the very most, it’ll fall 30%, still very impressive.

Michael continues to dominate, and plays more and more in parallel to The Passion of the Christ, which we’d pinned as the closest comparison since Michael’s first forecast. If Michael continues to follow this trajectory, it has a very good chance of clearing $400 million domestically.
